# Circuit Toy · instrument bench

Chosen with the user: dark instrument bench, bright wires, native glass controls.

The circuit is the hero. A graphite drafting field holds crisp cyan conductors, amber sources, labeled terminals, and real engineering symbols. Readouts sit beneath it with units and signs. Tapping a terminal probes its potential; tapping a part opens its values. Native Liquid Glass belongs to navigation and actions, while the schematic, formulas, plots, and lessons stay on opaque surfaces for clarity.

Three primary spaces: Bench (construct and measure), Labs (small causal experiments), Learn (the prerequisite map from charge to complex systems). Preserve Flips' immediate cause-and-effect, binary readouts, meaningful switch haptics, and explicit carry rather than copying its screens.

Every experiment uses the same rhythm: manipulate → measure → see the governing equation → try a prediction. Show model assumptions beside the experiment. Avoid decorative electron motion that could confuse electron drift with propagation or conventional current.

Use native SwiftUI navigation, sheets, segmented pickers, sliders and menus. Minimum iOS 27. iPhone first. Controls have at least 44-point targets, plots include accessible summaries, and transparency/motion preferences are respected. Build evidence does not establish physical haptic or device usability evidence.

Apple references: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials and https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/applying-liquid-glass-to-custom-views.
